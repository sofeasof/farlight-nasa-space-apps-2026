###**What does FARLIGHT do and how does it work?**
FARLIGHT is an interactive planetary archaeology experience designed to tell the stories of NASA machines left behind on the Moon and Mars. It transforms mission into an accessible digital exploration experience.

Visitors begin at the landing page, where they are invited to explore the archive and choose a planetary destination which is Mars or Moon. They can then discover artifacts associated with that world and investigate individual machines to uncover their stories.

The prototype currently features NASA's Ingenuity Mars Helicopter as its main investigation. Visitors can explore the helicopter through interactive components, discover the purpose of its engineering, follow its historic flights, learn about its contributions to the Perseverance mission, and uncover what happened during its final flight.

The experience follows a guided storytelling journey:
- Discover: Enter FARLIGHT and explore the planetary archive.
- Explore: Visit the Martian landscape and discover the Ingenuity helicopter.
- Investigate: Examine the helicopter's components and learn about its engineering and purpose.
- Remember: Follow its mission journey, achievements, final flight and lasting legacy.

###**What benefits does it provides?**
FARLIGHT offers several benefits:
- Makes complex information easier to understand: Visual exploration and simplified explanations help visitors understand the engineering and scientific achievements behind planetary exploration.
- Encourages active learning: Instead of passively reading information, visitors interact with artifacts and discover information at their own pace.
- Connects people with space exploration history: It brings attention to machines whose missions have ended but whose contributions remain significant.
- Supports informal education: Students, educators and casual space enthusiasts can use the experience to explore real NASA missions in a more engaging way.
- Preserves mission stories: By bringing historical information into an interactive digital experience, FARLIGHT provides a way to appreciate the legacy of these machines.
  
###**What is the intended impact?**
FARLIGHT aims to change how people perceive machines left behind on other worlds. These machines are not simply abandoned equipment; they are evidence of human curiosity, engineering achievements and scientific progress.

Through interactive storytelling, the project hopes to encourage greater interest in planetary exploration, help younger audiences appreciate the technology behind space missions, and highlight the scientific contributions of machines that continue to shape our understanding of other worlds.

In the future, FARLIGHT could expand into a larger digital archive featuring more NASA missions and artifacts from both the Moon and Mars, allowing visitors to investigate the stories of multiple planetary explorers.

###**Tools and Technologies were used**
The project was developed as an interactive web prototype using:

- Figma Make: Used to develop the interactive website experience, screen layouts, navigation and user interactions.
- Figma: Used for visual design, interface refinement and the overall presentation of the experience.
- NASA mission resources: Official NASA information and mission documentation were used as references for the historical and scientific content, particularly information about the Ingenuity Mars Helicopter.
- ChatGPT: Used as an AI-assisted tool for brainstorming, refining the storytelling approach, organising information and supporting the development process.
No additional hardware or custom physical components were required. The current submission demonstrates the concept through a digital prototype rather than a fully deployed application with a live database or backend.

###**How is the project creative and innovative?**
FARLIGHT approaches the challenge from the perspective of planetary archaeology. Instead of simply listing machines that remain on the Moon and Mars, it treats each machine as an artifact with a history worth investigating.

Its creativity lies in combining three approaches:
- Interactive archaeology: Visitors explore machines as artifacts, examining their components and discovering how they contributed to their missions.
Evidence-based storytelling: Real NASA mission information is presented through a narrative journey, connecting engineering details, mission milestones and scientific achievements.
- Emotional storytelling: The experience moves from curiosity and discovery to the machine's achievements, final moments and lasting legacy. It encourages visitors to appreciate the significance of a mission even after the machine can no longer operate.
This approach turns the challenge of remembering discarded equipment into an opportunity to celebrate the achievements behind humanity's exploration of other worlds.

###**Factors were considered during development**
Historical accuracy: Mission details and scientific information were based on NASA resources to ensure the stories remain grounded in documented events.
User experience: Clear navigation, return controls, interaction guidance and a guided journey were considered to help first-time visitors understand how to use the experience.

Accessibility and simplicity: Information was organised into manageable sections with visual elements to make technical subjects easier for general audiences to understand.
Visual storytelling: A cinematic space-museum aesthetic was chosen to create a sense of exploration while keeping the interface readable and focused on the artifacts.

Performance and usability: The experience was designed with screen fit, responsive layouts, smooth transitions and straightforward interactions in mind.
Scope and feasibility: Development focused on delivering a complete, understandable prototype around one main artifact, Ingenuity, rather than attempting to build an entire planetary archive within the hackathon timeframe.
