PROBLEM STATEMENT

Despite their significant contributions to space explorations, the stories of NASA's equipment left behind on the Moon and Mars can be difficult for school-age audiences and casual space enthusiasts to connect with and understand.

Information about these machines, including their original purposes, engineering, scientific doscoveries, and eventual mission outcomes, is distributed across technical records, mission archives, and historical documnetation.

Without an engaging and accessible way to connect these elements, audience may overlook the scientific achievements and lasting legacy of the machines that made planetary exploration possible.

There is opportunity to transform these scattered mission histories into meaningful and engaging storytelling experiences that help people understand not only what these machines were, but also what they accomplished and why their contributions matter.
